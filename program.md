# Iron Soviet Program

## Program Meta
- Title: ☭ Iron Soviet

## Exercises
- [pushup] **Push-Ups** 💪
  - Equipment: Bodyweight ± backpack
  - Slot: Push
  - Why: Shoulder-safe, no barbell needed
  - Instructions: Keep core tight. Lower until chest nearly touches ground. Add weight via backpack once bodyweight is too easy.
  - Tips: Hands just outside shoulders, Squeeze glutes to keep hips level, Full lockout at top
  - Image: Proper push-up form showing straight body alignment

- [kb_row] **Kettlebell Row** 👐
  - Equipment: Kettlebell
  - Slot: Pull
  - Why: No pull-up bar needed, protects low back
  - Instructions: One arm at a time. Brace core, pull elbow past torso, squeeze shoulder blade.
  - Tips: Flat back, slight hip hinge, Don't rotate torso, Controlled negative
  - Image: One-arm kettlebell row with flat back position

- [goblet_squat] **Goblet Squat** 🦵
  - Equipment: Kettlebell
  - Slot: Legs (squat)
  - Why: Teaches bracing, back-safe
  - Instructions: Hold kettlebell at chest. Squat deep, keep chest up, knees track over toes.
  - Tips: Elbows between knees at bottom, Drive through whole foot, Pause at bottom for control
  - Image: Goblet squat with kettlebell held at chest level

- [ham_curl] **Hamstring Curl** 🦵
  - Equipment: Gym machine
  - Slot: Legs (hinge)
  - Why: Direct posterior chain, zero spinal load
  - Instructions: Adjust machine pad to sit just above ankles. Curl with control, squeeze at top.
  - Tips: Don't arch your back, Slow negative (3 seconds), Full range of motion
  - Image: Seated or lying hamstring curl machine form

- [ohp] **Overhead Press (KB)** 🏋️
  - Equipment: Kettlebell
  - Slot: Shoulder
  - Why: Armpit at 45°, joint-safe neutral grip
  - Instructions: Start at shoulder. Press straight up with neutral grip. Brace core — don't arch back.
  - Tips: Armpit at 45° — NOT flared out, Lockout overhead, bicep by ear, Breathe at the top
  - Image: Kettlebell overhead press with neutral grip and 45-degree arm angle

- [dead_bug] **Dead Bug** 🎯
  - Equipment: Bodyweight
  - Slot: Core
  - Why: Anti-extension, no spinal compression
  - Instructions: Lie on back, arms up, knees at 90°. Extend opposite arm and leg. Keep lower back pressed into floor.
  - Tips: Exhale as you extend, Move slowly — this is a control exercise, If back lifts off floor, reduce range
  - Image: Dead bug exercise with opposite arm and leg extended

- [conditioning] **Row / Ski Erg** 🚣
  - Equipment: Gym machines
  - Slot: Conditioning
  - Why: Full-body conditioning, low injury risk
  - Instructions: 10-15 minutes total. Alternate between rower and ski erg, or pick one. Moderate steady pace.
  - Tips: Don't death-grip the handle, Push with legs first on rower, Keep consistent stroke rate
  - Image: Rowing machine and ski erg conditioning

## Schedule
- Monday: Heavy (Low reps · Long rest · Strength) | 4 sets | 3–5 reps | 2–3 min rest | Stop 2 reps before failure. Rest 2-3 min between sets. Choose a load where you could do 5-7 reps if forced.
- Wednesday: Volume (More reps · Shorter rest · Hypertrophy) | 3 sets | 8–12 reps | 90s rest | Stop 2 reps before failure. Rest 90s between sets. Controlled tempo, feel the muscle.
- Friday: Light (Low load · Perfect form · Speed) | 3 sets | 8–10 reps | 60s rest | Light or bodyweight only. Focus on perfect form and controlled tempo. Rest 60s.

## Rest Protocol
### 1. Move, but don't train
Low-intensity movement increases blood flow to muscles without creating fatigue, aiding repair.
- Walking outdoors (morning sunlight for circadian rhythm & vitamin D synergy)
- Very light rowing (conversational pace, no clock-watching)
- Casual cycling
Avoid anything that makes you breathe hard or creates muscle burn.

### 2. Hydration & salt
Muscle repair requires intracellular water. Without adequate sodium, you excrete water faster.
- Water across the day + pinch of salt in one glass or on food
- Urine stays pale yellow, not clear

### 3. Eat the full food checklist
Rest days are when muscle protein synthesis peaks from the prior workout. The checklist is **non-negotiable** even on off days.

### 4. Sleep optimization
Growth hormone pulses during deep sleep. One bad night measurably lowers muscle repair.
- Same bedtime as workout days

### 5. Walk after meals
10-minute walk after the largest meal improves nutrient partitioning — glucose is shuttled into muscles instead of sitting in the bloodstream.

### 6. Stretch or mobility
5–10 min of the same post-workout stretches. Do it while watching something — zero effort integration.

### 7. Do NOT
- Do extra sets "just because" — blunts recovery, increases injury risk
- Sit 8+ hours without standing breaks — undoes hip mobility gains
- Drink alcohol heavily — inhibits muscle protein synthesis for 24+ hours
- Skip meals to "save calories" — catabolic state on a rest day is counterproductive

## Never Do
- No cold stretching — warm up first
- No failure sets — always leave 2 in the tank
- No flared elbows on overhead press — armpit at 45°
- No high sugar drinks or desserts

## Troubleshooting
- [x] ❌ **Problem:** “Workout feels too easy or too hard.”
  - [ ] **Solution:** Your work capacity is adjusting. This is normal. If it feels easy, use a heavier weight or add a rep. If it feels too hard, reduce weight slightly. Listen to your body.

- [x] ❌ **Problem:** “I missed a workout or ate poorly yesterday.”
  - [ ] **Solution:** Do not compensate by over-training or fasting. Just get back on schedule today. Consistency beats intensity.

- [x] ❌ **Problem:** “My joints hurt during an exercise.”
  - [ ] **Solution:** Stop immediately. Do not push through pain. Try a lighter weight or a different variation (e.g., goblet squats instead of lunges). If pain persists, consult a professional.

- [x] ❌ **Problem:** “I’m not seeing progress.”
  - [ ] **Solution:** Track your lifts meticulously. Are you increasing weight or reps over time? If not, you may need to increase your calorie intake to support muscle growth. Ensure protein is sufficient (1g/lb bodyweight).

## Quick Reference
| Term | Meaning | Action | 
|------|---------|--------|
| **RPE** | Rate of Perceived Exertion — how hard the set feels | Stop when you could still do 2 more reps (about an 8 on a 1-10 scale) | 
| **Set** | A group of reps performed back-to-back | Rest 2-3 min between sets | 
| **Heavy Day** | Low reps (3-5), long rest (2-3 min) | Focus on strength and weight | 
| **Volume Day** | High reps (8-12), short rest (90s) | Focus on muscle size and work capacity | 
| **Light Day** | Moderate reps (8-10), very light or bodyweight-only | Focus on form, speed, and motor control | 
| **Progressive Overload** | Gradually increasing stress on the body | Add weight/reps/better form over time to force adaptation |
